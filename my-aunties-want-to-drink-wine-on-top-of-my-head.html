My Aunties Want to Drink Wine on My Head, so I Sent Them a Bottle

I am only 26, and yet my aunties seem to believe my "marriage star" is dimming.

5 min read Life

"You know, as a woman, no matter the wealth you amass or the educational heights you get to, without a man, you will never be complete."

"Nne, you have to know that a woman has stars that shine at different times of her life, and there's an age you'll get to when that star will start dimming. At that age, it becomes very hard to find a husband. You are nearing that age."

"When will we come and drink wine on your head? Your elder sister has gone to her husband's house; it's your turn now."

Apparently, I've Got a Marriage Star—That's Dimming by the Way

I think my family members want to shove marriage down my throat. To them, it's the highest achievement a woman can have.

To me, it is just another accessory you can do without, and I will say that anywhere, anytime, any day.

I am the fourth child of my parents, the second girl. See, I am only 26, and yet my aunties seem to believe my "marriage" star is dimming.

I have never been able to come to terms with the fact that a woman "needs" a man to finally attain a level of completeness. I don't see why another being, outside yourself, should be the one to make you whole. Why do my aunties think a woman's life only properly starts when a man validates her?

Let's go by the picture my aunt painted. As a woman, I have a good education and I am well placed financially. I have the means and ease of taking care of core aspects of life that indeed require resources—like living healthy, being able to make an impact in society, curating an environment rid of chaos and hardship, and supporting my loved ones in their endeavours. Let us add to that faith or belief in whatever higher being you choose, for those who indulge. I don't see where the man is "needed."

Yes, having a partner with whom one shares companionship and love can be a beautiful and healthy thing, if the people involved keep it from toxicity. A step further from that, a woman choosing to settle down with a partner in order to start a family is a wonderful thing.

But I said "choosing," not "having to" or "being forced to." That right there is the difference to me. It is not compulsory to get married or have kids.

There is nothing I have not heard as a rebuttal to this. This was one of them:

Friend: "Are you a Christian?"

Me: "Yes."

Friend: "If you are a Christian, you should know that's a sin against God because the Bible said, 'Be fruitful and multiply.'"

He continued:

"If you, as a Christian woman, decide to not get married or have children, you are sinning against and turning your back on your God!"

Or is it the "you will die alone and miserable" or "who will inherit your legacy" shtick? I find it incredible that in 2026, we still have people harassing others who have chosen to be unmarried or—even a worse crime to them—childfree. And that is where I have a problem.

When Society Says "next!"

Growing up and finding myself taught me to put things under the microscope. It made me ask questions: Do I really believe this, or is this what they want me to believe? Do I really want this, or is this what they want me to have? Do I really enjoy this, or am I only doing this because I'm expected to?

I don't want to do things because it's the next step according to society: High school —> get your degree —> get a job —> get married —> make babies.

And I certainly don't want to do things because I'm automatically expected to because of culture or faith. C'mon, as an adult?

Different people have different priorities in life, and honestly, marriage isn't one of mine.

Today, I am 26, and nothing feels better than the feeling of liberation knowing that I can hold views of my own and stand by them. I know things were different in the time of our mothers and grandmothers, but times have changed, and women have come so far from those days.

Send Them Wine Instead

My mother was the first to embrace the fact that, of all her children, I was different. She stopped fighting back certain views of mine with "blood of Jesus" and "holy ghost fire." She stopped threatening to haul me to one man of God or the other—which, by the way, never worked. And when I'd make certain statements about things like marriage, she'd simply give me the look we both knew said, "Hmm, okay. I may not agree exactly, but you do you," and I loved her for that.

So, when my sister's traditional marriage came around, bringing my aunties and other relatives in tow, I knew I was in for some nagging.

You should see their faces when I responded to their marriage "advice" with blatant turndowns. I had grown quite used to it at that point. I have had women in church try to do some matchmaking on my head. So, I was quite comfortable with giving them proper responses by then.

So, the next time I was told, "We want to come and drink wine on your head," I sent them a bottle of wine.
