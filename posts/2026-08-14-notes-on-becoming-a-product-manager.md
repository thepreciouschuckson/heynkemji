## My career origin story. Or is it?

It must be about a year since I started talking about my pivot into tech, particularly Product Management.

This, honestly, has been a long time coming. After dabbling from social media management to full-time digital marketing, then VA, and then eventually Executive Assistance—managing two to three clients at most times—I knew it was time to do something that actually felt like a career and not a hustle.

For a time, that 'career' looked like it would be in Comms. That started after I got the opportunity to fill a Media & Comms Officer role. At that time, it honestly felt like the stars had aligned and brought me to where I was always meant to be.

It felt perfect, just the right thing for me. Well suited to my skills and 'interests'. In fact, to think it came when I was getting my BSc in Mass Communication and Media Studies.

The stars indeed seemed to have aligned. I would say this to my friends, and anyone who cared to listen. I was confident, I was certain. And yes, for a time, that seemed to have been going well.

I loved my job, my workplace, and my bosses. It was an enabling environment; at first, it seemed like I could do anything.

Then, things started to feel off. I couldn't say for sure at the time that something was wrong with my 'love' for my work in Comms. I was learning stuff, I was doing stuff. And then, once again, my health decided to pull the brakes on this important thing in my life. I had a health crisis.

Oh, have I mentioned at any point that I am epileptic? I guess that'd be a topic for another day.

Back to the brakes. I had to resign. I just couldn't. I ended up in the hospital—again. And that is how my Comms life at Teach the Child Initiative ended.

Let's skip the gap in between all of that happening in 2024. Again, pretty sure my life update will be a topic for another day: I ended up up and moving countries, only to return months after, and hating myself for it.

## A second chance that should probably have never been.

For the sake of this piece, let's assume the previous paragraph handled everything that happened after I had to leave my Comms role.

Sometime in 2025, by the time I returned to Nigeria, my boss at Teach The Child, Pearl Utuk, sought me out again and offered to work with me in the same capacity as Media and Comms Officer. I was elated. I felt useful, needed (or at least, wanted). Again, it seemed that the stars had aligned so well, giving me another chance. 

At that time, it had already been cemented into my heart that Comms was my thing. I was looking for Comms roles, I talked about myself as a Comms professional. I was learning, seeking ways to improve myself as a Comms professional. My LinkedIn feed was filled with Comms people and posts. There's no way you'd tell me this wasn't for me.

So, back to work. The people loved me. We were working on a new project and I was eager to jump in—which I was required to do, btw, since I came onboard mid-project and they needed me to hit the ground running.

And I did. For a month. And then, things started to cool off, gradually. By month two into three, things became a drag. Simple tasks would seem so difficult. Social media was dying, I was dying (my Comms life, to be more precise). My bosses were noticing, they were concerned. When they brought me back onboard, they did so with consideration for my health. 

They were understanding, they'd even give me leeway to work from home on the days I couldn't make it in. I took it. And took more. Days when I had to show up in the office, unintentionally, I would somehow always arrive late. 

> Their patience was wearing thin, rightly so. I was beginning to hate myself. And then, I started flaring up. 

My doctors guessed it was the pressure from work, and boy did I feel pressured by work! I was grossly underperforming, taking too many workdays off, and my work was suffering—not just my role, but the entire organization. I couldn't bear it. 

Board members and other higher-ups of the organization came in physically for a review meeting. It was supposed to be a review of everything going on with the project, but it ended up being a review of me and my department, Comms. It was obvious there was barely anything from my end, and being a core role, the project was suffering. 

I recall one of the higher-ups trying to point me to ideas on how to make content better and all; my boss interrupted, looked me dead in the eye, and went, 'Precious isn't lacking ideas'—basically, she knew something was wrong with me and it was beyond knowing what to 'post.'

I felt naked as they all looked at me. She was right; something was deeply wrong with me.

And once again, this time feeling like a dirty cop-out, my health got worse and, being the log of wood serving no other purpose than tripping people up, I resigned. Utterly ashamed and feeling like a failure—which, honestly, I was in that regard.

I knew something had broken in me and I just never could go back to Comms. And I mean, never. Gun to my head right now, I still can't say what exactly happened to me, but I knew something broke inside me.

## They say there's only one way to go when you're down—up. It didn't really feel like it.

I was jobless for more than 8 months. How I survived those eight months, I do not know.

Fast forward to sometime in April—the 7th, to be precise. I received an email that I had been accepted into some mentorship program that I had forgotten I even applied to. I got in. I applied under the Product Management track. My mentor, Ayobami Awode—a Chief of Staff at HerVest, who certainly wasn't a product manager—asked me and my co-mentee if we wanted to move forward with her, knowing she wasn't some product manager. I decided to take it; I mean, who knows what this could hold for me.

She turned out to be a pretty chill lady from the bits and pieces I was able to glean about her personal life from our sessions. To my advantage, my co-mentee missed most of our sessions, so I had the benefit of enjoying 1:1s, and I can say that served me in the best way possible.

She, on two occasions, brought in her friend who is a senior PM to have sessions with us: the first being a technical session on product thinking, and the second being questions and real-life sharing of her own story and how she got into PM. I recall that being a particularly insightful session. I should remember her name; I'll check.

Anyway, from CV reviews that showed me my CV had been a** all along to encouraging me to ditch my insanely verbose 'portfolio' for a better one, which led me to create a portfolio website that could actually hold water, that was the beginning of my actual pivot.

Months before then, I had been taking courses, even though I left them unfinished. But at that time, I had a pretty good foundational grasp of this PM business. It excited me, the work and everything that would come with it.

So, when I had the push to actually work on my portfolio, that was one process I enjoyed and was thoroughly proud of. I believe that was the first time I had anything to do with the world of coding. I was introduced to VS Code, GitHub, HTML, and CSS. For the first time, the coloured lines of code I used to see on Brian's screen started to make sense. I no longer saw gibberish. Pause. I didn't learn to write code in that short while; I leveraged AI-assisted web development. I would work with Gemini to generate the code, then take it to VS Code and do the work from there.

I was exhilarated. I was doing that. I mean, I had the option to do drag-and-drop, but chose to see what happened behind the scenes. That meant something to me. It meant a lot.

For the first time in a while, I felt like I had stepped up... all the way into the world of actual tech. LOL

By the time I had finished working on my portfolio, which in the process afforded me the time to work on a couple of projects for said portfolio, I had learnt plenty and was better positioned.

And finally, I moved from 'I am pivoting' to 'I have pivoted' to Product Management.

All this happened before the end of the 8-week mentorship program with Ayobami, which was one of my goals for the mentorship. That was a win. She was excited and proud of me. I had a PM CV put together; I had edited my LinkedIn profile to reflect the shift. I was a Product Manager.

But the real deal was yet to be addressed. How do I actually get real-world experience? How do I get an internship? Or even a volunteer role or junior role? Anything; I just wanted to work.

## Maybe stars aligning are really a thing.

Then one day, I met with a former teacher and friend who, over lunch, told me about a product he was working on. It was a brilliant idea. I loved it immediately, but beyond that, I saw my opening. I identified that he had a lean team and he was basically operating an "idea-to-dev" system. I immediately pitched him on the spot why he needed a PM on his team to properly get myStaffroom, the product he was building, off the ground.

It took a bit, but he eventually bought it, and there and then, I had engineered my own internship.

I came in basically as a founding PM with all the excitement of a newbie, and transformed this 'idea-to-dev' system into a proper agile system. Even though that was yet to be my first use of Jira, I pitched that we employ its use and thankfully, in all this, the dev I was going to be working with was a lovely lady: dear Zainab Ogunola.

I immediately saw holes, talked through them with my boss and the dev, and in no time, what looked like optimization of just a few features before launch in a couple of weeks turned into a full-blown optimization where the product had been reimagined and developed far beyond the vision of the founder.

I loved the job. I loved the thrill of it. I loved understanding the jargon, the product thinking, the work in the background. I loved loved loved it all. If indeed stars aligning are a thing, then safe to say the stars had never aligned as much as they did for me with my pivot into Product Management.

I was learning on the job, incredibly. I leveraged the AI tools I had to learn and grow. Things started to make sense to me all the more. And three months in, the 'honeymoon' continues.

I am still looking for the opportunity to work in a bigger team or at least one where I will have a senior PM to report to and learn from. Finding an opportunity has been impossible, but I've got a bird in hand.

V1 of myStaffroom launches on Sept 4, 2026, finally. And I am indeed proud of all the work that has brought me here.

If this were a book, on my acknowledgements page would come the name Brian Iyoha. The one person who engaged with me all through figuring out which tech track I wanted to go for, down to giving me access to a paid course, hearing my rants, explaining stuff to me, and basically being my tech big bro (ew lol). But really, that nigga is a real one.

> And oh, I know this won't see the same fate as the career I thought I was going to have in Comms. I feel this thrill, but trust me, it's different. Let's just say, I know my stars better now.
