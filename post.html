Notes on becoming a product manager

Nobody hands you a title and a finished playbook at the same time. Some things I've had to work out by getting them wrong first.

6 min read Product Career

When I started, I thought the job was mostly about writing specs and running meetings well. It took a few shipped features, and a few that never should have shipped, to understand that the actual job is deciding what not to build.

Most of my early mistakes came from skipping the boring part: talking to the people who would actually use the thing before I had an opinion about how it should work. It's tempting to start with a solution, especially when you can see one clearly. The discipline is holding off.

What actually changed my process

Three shifts made the biggest difference. First, writing the problem down before the feature — one paragraph, no jargon, readable by someone outside the team. Second, treating every launch as a hypothesis instead of a finish line. Third, asking for feedback earlier than felt comfortable, before the work looked finished enough to defend.

The work isn't the deck or the roadmap. It's the judgment you bring to deciding what's worth either.

None of this came from a course, though the courses helped me name what I was already doing wrong. It came from shipping something, watching how people actually used it, and being willing to say the first version missed.

What I'm still working on

Saying no earlier. Writing shorter specs. Trusting a smaller test before committing to a bigger build. None of it is solved — this is more of a running log than a conclusion.
