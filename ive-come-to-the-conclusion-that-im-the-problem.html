I've Come to the Conclusion That I'm the Problem

On trying to cultivate a sense of style at twenty-six, broken slippers in Wuse 2, and surrendering my wardrobe to my brother.

4 min read Life

Deep sigh...

Fashion, makeup, skin care...three things pretty commonly known to be on the front burner for every other girl in her twenties.

Over the years, I will admit that my sense of style has improved. I have come to desire actually looking stylish. I have come to intentionally want to try to achieve these fashion goals. I guess it just hasn't been top of mind for me, but with age has come change.

Y'know, there are dates to show up for, meetings to attend, parties to go for, and even the occasional dancing all night at clubs. Yeah, yeah, you can be as 'I don't care' as they come, but trust me, even the 'I don't do too much' people still do something.

In fact, it's the people who put in the least effort and still come out looking as good as it gets that I envy. The Italians call it "sprezzatura" — the art of making something difficult look easy; effortless grace that maintains that nonchalant demeanor yen yen yen *rolls eyes

So, I'm twenty-six. And I have no sense of style. I actually have to try. I actually have to put in some thought. I don't think I have this sprezzatura.

Thankfully, my brother is a fashion designer with a good eye for wardrobe curation.

I think not having the interest early on affected my possession of actual wardrobe items. So, when I have to show up, it's a mad frenzy of what do I do? What do I put together? There is no effortless 'grab anything and you're good.'

Take this fateful weekend. I was to go partying with a friend. This person insisted on me getting a dress for this particular occasion. I started searching from 4pm. I traipsed from store to store looking for a dress. I even cut my slippers in the process, further heightening my frustration.

You should have seen me, one slipper in hand and of all times, I wasn't close to any mart I could buy flipflops from. In Wuse 2 for that matter!

Nothing seemed to work. If it wasn't a colour I was complaining of, it was the glitter or the cut. Just something! I ended up finding a store I felt held some promise and found my way there by 8pm, calling several times and begging they stayed open for me.

It was a most horrifyingly stressful day. By the end of the day, I managed to find a dress that mind you, I'd rejected without trying and only came to consider it after trying on almost ten outfits.

It started pouring so heavily like even the elements got tired of me.

Yes, I know I have very weirdly specific preferences that are clearly paralyzing my fashion upgrade efforts. I don't like bright colours, no glitter or sequins, no busy designs, no shiny (especially gold) clips...it goes on and on. And we all know how that is eliminating at least 70 percent of the clothing items in Nigerian shopping malls (boutiques). But for real, what's with you people and busy designs?

I can bet anyone else would have found a dress in less than half the time I spent searching that weekend.

And that's just one such event.

Did I mention my brother? Yes! We live together and thankfully, he's been my stylist. Best believe if I come out looking so good, he put my clothes together 😭

I try to contest his decisions sometimes and lord knows how horribly wrong I turn out to be.

So, I'm back here again, today. At the time of writing this, I have handed him my phone to pick clothes for me, style them in his head and make all these calls for me. And no, I am not ashamed to say I had spent two hours prior doing the exact task I have just begged him to do.

Sigh, I am the problem. But I'm trying to fix it, I promise. That's why I decided to get clothes anyway.

p.s for someone who's looking forward to her 30s, is this the style we'd carry into our rich aunty era? Hell nah.

Girl, better dress up!
